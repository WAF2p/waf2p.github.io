The Wombat brand

One wombat, one hard hat, eight pillars. This is the visual system behind Wombat: a vector mascot that explains, a monochrome icon set for the framework, and a terminal that keeps the whole thing honest. Everything on this page is downloadable.

A mascot with a job.

The wombat wears a hard hat because the work is foundations: controls, evidence, exit strategies. The head is the mark. The six expressions cover the tone of voice — use them to clarify a sentence, never as decoration.

The whole wombat, six ways.

The head is the mark; the body is the character. These painterly PNGs carry the same control-room backdrop, so the wombat can point, dig, explain or celebrate without changing style.

Full-body PNGs, 928×1152, with the control-room backdrop included. WebP variants are used on this page.

Eight pillars, eight icons.

One shape per pillar, drawn on a 24×24 grid with a 1.75 stroke so they sit next to Lucide icons without arguing. They inherit currentColor, which means one file works in light and dark mode. No mascot inside the icon — the framework gets its own language.

The rules we actually keep.

A mascot earns its place by being useful. These are the lines we do not cross.

Do

  • Give the mascot a job: let it point at the thing the reader should look at.
  • Keep the hard hat yellow and the outline dark. The hat is the constant.
  • Match the expression to the sentence — idle for calm, alert for risk.
  • Leave one brim-height of clear space around the head.
  • Use the pillar icons for framework concepts; they are drawn to work at 24px.

Don't

  • Recolor, stretch, rotate or crop the mark.
  • Put the wombat in a suit. It is a builder.
  • Use the mascot as a bullet or a loading spinner — that is what the icons are for.
  • Place the head on busy artwork without a solid surface behind it.
  • Use hard-hat yellow for buttons. That is brand blue's job.
